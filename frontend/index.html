        ▸ Futures curve structure · calendar spread matrix (current snapshot)
        T7

        Calendar Spread Matrix — P(col) − P(row), $/bbl

        live, every 3 min

        Each cell is the $-difference between the column's contract price and the row's contract price. Green = column richer (contango leg); red = column cheaper (backwardation leg). True covariance would need multi-week historical curve snapshots; this is the structural snapshot.

        T7B

        Calendar Spread Covariance Matrix ($²/bbl², equal-weighted)

        —

        Plain sample covariance matrix between consecutive calendar spreads (M1-M2, M2-M3, ..., M11-M12) in $²/bbl² units. Diagonal = sample variance of each spread (= spread vol²). Off-diagonal = pairwise covariance. Equal-weighted across all snapshots, Bessel-corrected (n−1) — the literal covariance matrix in the statistical sense (no EWMA decay). Color: red = positive (spreads co-move), green = negative (anti-correlated, good for fly trades). Adjacent spreads share a common month so they're typically anti- correlated, which is why a butterfly trade (var(fly) = var(M1-M2) + var(M2-M3) − 2·cov(M1-M2, M2-M3)) usually has HIGHER variance than the sum of leg variances — that's the hidden risk in fly structures.

        Product Curves — WTI · Brent · RBOB · HO · NatGas (real 12-month settlements)

        ▸ Phase 2 — Regime-based analytical engine · fingerprint · models · opportunities
        P2A

        Current Regime Fingerprint — 5-Dimension Classification

        —

        Five independent dimensions, each binned into 3-5 buckets. The tuple of bucket labels is the "regime fingerprint" — two markets sharing the same fingerprint should behave similarly. Inventory z vs trailing 60-day. Volatility = z of 20-day realized vol vs trailing 60-day of realized vols. Seasonality from ISO week-of-year (winter heating / spring shoulder / summer driving / fall turnaround). Curve = M12−M1 slope buckets. Macro = DXY 20-day percent change buckets. Historical observations are bucketed by these same definitions, so the regime fingerprint is both the classification and the lookup key.

        REGIME

        Per-Product Percentile Regime — quintile labels from each product's own slope history

        —

        Each product's M12-M1 slope distribution gets its own 20/40/60/80 percentile cutoffs — so Q1 means "in the bottom 20% of this product's slope history" (deepest backwardation FOR THIS PRODUCT), and Q5 means top 20% (deepest contango). Shown alongside the legacy hard-cutoff label (Steep Back / Back / Flat / Contango / Steep Contango) — those cutoffs were calibrated for WTI in $/bbl and produce 90%+ class imbalance on LGO ($/mt) and HO ($/gal). When the two schemes disagree, the percentile label is the more meaningful one for that product. NO ML used — pure labeling.

        PSTRAT

        Paper Strategies — PCA Curve / Bertram OU / HMM Regime (winners of the shootout)

        —

        Backtested winners of the 6-way shootout (PCA Curve + 5 published papers). PCA Curve auto-trades CL/LCO/LGO via the 3-tenor PC3 fly when |PC3 z| ≥ 2. Bertram OU trades HO calendar spreads when deviation crosses the OU-derived a* threshold. HMM Regime trades the WTCL log spread when posterior regime probability > 0.7 AND |z| > 1. Combined OOS Sharpe averaged across products: ~2.5. Auto-traded as source=pstrat with 1.5% stop / 72h time-stop / +3% take-profit. MONITOR tier shows current readings even when no trade fires.

        BPP

        Best-Per-Product Strategy — each product trades only its specialist model

        —

        For each product, the model family that wins the MOST targets in the 138-target backtest gets the steering wheel. We only show signals where that specialist model was the winner AND test R² ≥ 0.10. Up to 3 trades per product (caps single-product concentration). Auto-traded as source=bestpp in the paper book (1.8% stop / 96h time-stop / +3.5% take-profit).

        FUSION

        Composite Strategy — multi-factor weighted signal (regression + LGBM + technical + news + regime + macro)

        —

        Fuses every working engine on the dashboard into ONE per-product verdict. Each engine votes −1 / 0 / +1. Composite score = weighted sum (weights: real-data 25%, live LGBM 20%, technical 15%, news 10%, regime 10%, macro 10%, regression 10%). Verdict: LONG if score ≥ +0.30, SHORT if ≤ −0.30, else FLAT. HIGH conviction at |score| ≥ 0.50 (3 lots), MED at ≥ 0.30 (2 lots). HIGH/MED verdicts are auto-traded as source=composite in the paper book (2% stop / 96h time-stop / +4% take-profit).

        REAL

        Real-Curve Signals — your CL/LCO/LGO/HO/WTCL M1-M14 (LGBM/XGBoost/Huber + 60/20/20)

        —

        Trained on your 5 uploaded xlsx files (~1,500-1,750 daily bars per product, M1-M14 curves). 60/20/20 chronological split (train/val/test). 6 model families tested per target (Linear, Ridge, Lasso, ElasticNet, Huber, LGBM, XGBoost); winner is the one with the highest validation R² and the displayed test R² is fully out-of-sample. Macro features (REAL, no synthetic): lagged returns, DXY 5d, 20d realized vol, curve slope (M12-M1), curve curvature (M3-2M6+M9). HIGH confidence = test R² ≥ 0.30 (rare, real edge). MED = 0.10-0.30. LOW = 0.05-0.10. NOISE = below 0.05 (hidden). Working-effect tag: weighted_mid pricing on flies/spreads can introduce mechanical mean reversion (Working 1960). When the underlying series has lag-1 diff autocorrelation worse than −0.05, we show ⚠ WE next to R² and the haircut-R² (live realistic value). ✓ clean means the signal is fully tradable. Tip: LGO and LGBM-fitted outrights are typically clean; CL/LCO flies tend to carry a 20-35% mechanical haircut.

        LIVE

        Trading Signals — 5 products + 3 spreads (LGBM, next-5d outlook)

        —

        Live signal stack from 5-year daily LGBM fits on each product and three desk spreads (WTI−Brent, 321Crack proxy, RBOB−HO). Signal = LONG if predicted next-5d move > +0.5% (or threshold for spreads), SHORT if < −0.5%, else FLAT. Confidence tier (HIGH/MED/LOW) reflects each strategy's out-of-sample historical Sharpe and win rate — HIGH means the model has earned its trust on past data, LOW means take with a grain of salt. Refits every 6 hours.

        P2B

        Regression Model Comparison — Linear / Ridge / Lasso / ElasticNet / Huber / LGBM (per regime)

        —

        For each curve regime with sufficient observations (n ≥ 5), we fit 6 regression families on (features → next-tick fly): LinearRegression (baseline), Ridge α=1.0 (L2), Lasso α=0.05 (L1, sparse), ElasticNet α=0.05 / l1_ratio=0.5 (blend), Huber (robust to outliers), and LGBM (gradient-boosted trees, captures non-linearities the linear families cannot). Reported: train R², out-of-sample test R² on last 20%, MAE, and # non-zero coefficients (for LGBM, # features with non-zero split gain). Winner per regime = highest test R². The exercise isn't to find one universal model; it's to surface which family wins where. Features: [fly_now, WTI 5d/20d return, DXY 5d return, BB position, inventory-z, vol-z, slope].

        P2C

        Ranked Analytical Opportunities — regime-conditional deviations

        —

        For every (product × structure) pair — 5 products × 5 structures (fly, M1-M2, M3-M6, M6-M9, slope) — we compute the deviation of the live value from its regime-conditional historical mean. Each comparison uses the per-dimension stats from the Phase 2 historical database. Score = |average z| × confidence (log-scaled by sample size) × robustness (1 − std of z's across dimensions; high when the deviation is consistent across all 5 lenses, low when it's only signaled by one). Ranked top-N shown. WTI rows have full regime-conditional stats from rolling `curve_hist`; other products surface current values only until their curve history accumulates.

        P2D

        Historical Regime Database — per-dimension summary

        —

        Per-dimension bucketed stats: for each of the 5 dimensions, walks every historical curve snapshot, assigns it to a bucket, and summarizes the distribution of fly / M1-M2 / M3-M6 / M6-M9 / slope within each bucket — count n, mean, std, 10/50/90 quantiles. The "what's typical here" reference set used by the Opportunity Engine. Refresh every 5 min.

        P3B

        —

        Highest-conviction trades across all 5 products. z = (actual − predicted) / RMSE. Strong +z = spread is rich vs the model → SHORT it (mean-revert down). Strong −z = cheap → LONG it. Lots = round(clip(|z| × R² × 2.5, 1, 5)) — sizing weights model confidence into the position. Only structures with R² ≥ 0.30 generate actionable trades; everything else is shown in P3C as WATCH-only. Direction colors: green = LONG, red = SHORT, grey = WATCH.

        P3C

        Regime-Wise Trade Book — per product, current regime + best ideas

        —

        Each product card shows: (1) its current curve regime classified from the live M1–M12 slope (steep_backwardation / backwardation / flat / contango / steep_contango); (2) the top 5 trade ideas across its calendar spreads and flies, with model name, R², direction, lot size and the rationale (z-score). Per-product trades are grouped by the regime the product is currently in — so under a contango regime, all WTI spreads / flies that the models flag as mispriced get surfaced together as one coherent regime trade book.

        P3D

        Full Strip — every spread & fly, every product, level + change models

        —

        The raw model deck. One row per (product, structure, kind): spread or fly, level or next-day change. Click a product to filter. Level models trade rich/cheap mean-reversion on the spread itself. Change models attempt to predict the next-day move — far harder, so R² is mostly < 0.3 (the financially honest result: daily changes of spreads are close to a random walk). Where a change-model R² is high (e.g. WTCL M3–M4 change at 0.41 from Huber), it's a real timing edge.

        ▸ Phase 5 · News-aware trade signals · FinBERT sentiment × keyword routing × z-score fusion
        P5

        News-Aware Trade Signals — sentiment fused with BB / fly z-scores

        —

        For each oil-complex product the engine routes recent news (last 24h) via headline keyword matching, scores each headline with FinBERT (preferred) or VADER (fallback), adds a ±impact bias for supply-shock / weak-demand keywords, and aggregates to a single news vote per product. That vote is then summed with a z-score vote built from the dashboard's existing BB position and fly z. Combined vote in [−2, +2] maps to LONG/SHORT/HOLD with conviction tier and lot sizing. Entry / TP / SL use the same vol-banded formula as P3F and P4D (TP = entry + dir × 2σ × √5, SL = entry − dir × 1.5σ → R:R ≈ 2.98:1). Each product row expands to show the top 3 highest-|score| headlines that moved the verdict and the exact bull/bear/neutral counts.

        P3F

        Per-Product Trade Plan — buy/sell + entry / TP / SL with lot sizing

        —

        For each of the 5 oil-complex front-month products: the TA engine's direction call (LONG / SHORT / HOLD), suggested lot size, and a concrete trade plan derived from 20-day realized log-return vol:
        • Entry = current price · σ_$ = |price| × vol₂₀ (1-day $ std)
        • Take Profit = entry + dir × 2σ × √5 (a 5-day 2-sigma favorable move)
        • Stop Loss = entry − dir × 1.5σ (1-day 1.5-sigma adverse)
        • R:R ≈ 2√5 / 1.5 = 2.98:1 by construction. Lots auto-halved when daily vol > 5% (high-stress regimes). Same vol-banded TP/SL formula as panel P4D so trade plans are comparable across the regime and TA engines.

        P3E

        Technical-Analysis Trade Verdict — RSI · BB · EMA-cross · Momentum

        —

        Each product gets four equal-weight TA sub-signals voting −1 / 0 / +1: RSI(14) (oversold <30 = long, overbought >70 = short), BB(20, 2σ) (price below lower band = long), EMA(20/50) crossover (20>50 = trend long), SMA(20) momentum (price above SMA = long). Raw sum lives in [−4, +4]. |raw| ≥ 2 → trade with lots = min(5, max(1, |raw|)); otherwise HOLD (signals mixed). This is intentionally independent of the regression / regime engines so it serves as a second opinion you can override or stack with. Confidence % = fraction of the 4 sub-signals aligned with the verdict direction.

        ▸ Phase 4 · Persistent regime detection · HMM vs GMM vs KMeans fit-off across ~1,550 daily bars (2021–2026) · seasonality decomposition
        P4A

        Method Fit-Off — which regime classifier should you trust?

        —

        —

        Three families fit on the same feature stack (log-return, 20d realized vol, M1–M12 spread, M3–2M6+M9 fly). HMM (Gaussian emissions) models the time-ordered SEQUENCE and penalizes transitions — gives persistent, tradeable regimes. GMM is probabilistic clustering with no time dependence — relabels every time features drift across a cluster boundary, so it flickers (LGO: 91 switches, WTCL: 277 switches in ~1,550 bars = quasi-weekly flips). KMeans wins silhouette because it optimizes cluster tightness, but ignores time. Persistence (HMM only) is Pr(state[t+1] = state[t]) — when this is >0.95 you can hold a regime trade without getting bounced out by noise.

        P4B

        HMM State Characteristics — what each persistent regime actually means

        —

        Each HMM state is a Gaussian cluster in (return, vol, spread, fly) space, but tied together by the persistence prior. The signature columns show what each state looks like: mean log-return, mean 20-day realized vol, mean curve spread. Calm + tight vol + sign of spread ≈ a backwardation or contango regime; high vol + extreme spread ≈ a stress / dislocation regime. Share = % of history spent in that state. "Now" highlights the regime classified at the last training bar.

        P4C

        STL Seasonality — calendar-driven layer separate from regimes

        —

        Seasonal-trend-loess decomposition isolates the recurring calendar component (positive bars = month is seasonally rich, negative = seasonally cheap). Strength ≈ 0–1 ratio of seasonal variance to total variance; near 0.1 means seasonality is weak — expected for global benchmarks where storage arbitrage smooths the front month. Regime and seasonality are deliberately separate layers — regimes answer "what state are we in now?" while seasonality answers "what does this time of year usually do?". Both can be true at once.

        P4D

        Regime + Seasonality Trade Signals — buy/sell per product with lot sizing

        —

        Combines two independent votes per product into one verdict: (1) Regime drift vote — sign of the current HMM state's mean log-return, weighted by persistence (98%+ for LGO, 95%+ for WTCL = high conviction); (2) Seasonality vote — sign of the current calendar month's STL component (negative = seasonally cheap → LONG, positive = seasonally rich → SHORT). When both votes agree, conviction is HIGH and lots scale up to 5. When they disagree, the stronger weighted vote wins but lots are capped at 2. When the regime is high-vol (vol20 > 5%), lots are automatically halved — small position in stress regimes. Each row explains the why, the asset to actually trade, and the suggested direction × size.
